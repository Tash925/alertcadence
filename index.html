The problem

It's not a threat-comms problem. It's a translation problem.

Small businesses don't read CVEs. They skim. Security advisories are written for engineers — full of jargon, severity scores, and exploit detail that means nothing to the person who owns the bakery.

AlertCadence translates. It takes the same advisories a security team reads and turns each one into three plain sentences: what happened, why it matters to your business, and what's being done about it.

A raw advisory

  • "Path traversal via crafted request"
  • Severity 9.8, CWE-22
  • Client asks "do we need to worry?"

After AlertCadence

  • "An attacker could reach files they shouldn't"
  • "Your customer data could be exposed"
  • "We're patching and watching it now"

How it works

From raw feed to client-ready brief.

Four steps, all running on your own hardware. No cloud API, no account, nothing to leak.

01CISA KEV

Fetch

Pulls CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities feed — every item is already confirmed exploited in the wild.

02Python

Filter

Keeps the last 30 days and the vendors small businesses actually run. Ransomware-linked items rank first.

03Gemma · local

Summarize

A local open model rewrites each advisory into What happened / Why it matters / What we're doing.

04HTML

Send

Renders one clean, white-labeled brief you review and send. (PDF, email & scheduling on the roadmap.)

Every item is already confirmed-exploited
Runs on CPU — no GPU required
No data ever leaves your machine
